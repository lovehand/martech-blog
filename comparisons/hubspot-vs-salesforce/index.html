HubSpot vs Salesforce (2026): Which CRM Should You Buy?

HubSpot is the better pick for most small and midsize teams. Salesforce is the better pick when you need deep customization, complex sales processes or enterprise controls. Here is how they compare on features, real pricing at 5 and 25 users, ease of use, integrations and support.

Short answer: choose HubSpot if you want a CRM your team will actually use within a week, with marketing, sales and service tools that share one database. Choose Salesforce if you have a complex sales process, a dedicated admin (or budget for a partner) and need a platform you can shape around almost any workflow. For a team of 5, both can start cheaply. For a team of 25 on mid tier plans, the gap narrows and the decision comes down to fit, not price.

Quick comparison

Both are excellent CRMs. They are just built for different buyers. HubSpot started as a marketing platform and grew into sales and service. It is designed to be easy. Salesforce started as a sales tool for big companies and became a platform you can build on. It is designed to be flexible.

So the real question isn't "which is better?" It is "which one matches how my team sells, and who is going to run it?"

Where HubSpot pulls ahead

HubSpot's big advantage is that everything lives in one place and looks the same. Your marketing emails, landing page form fills, sales sequences, calls and support tickets all attach to the same contact record. A rep can open a contact and see which blog post that person read, which email they clicked and which ticket they opened last month. No syncing between products.

It also includes things that Salesforce often sells separately, like calling minutes, meeting links and a lot of marketing tooling. For a small team that wants one bill and one login, that matters.

Where Salesforce pulls ahead

Salesforce is a platform first. You can create custom objects, custom apps, complex approval chains, territory rules and permission models that HubSpot simply wasn't designed for. If your deals involve multiple products, partners, regions and approval steps, Salesforce can model that.

The new 2026 editions also bundle a lot. Salesforce now says Core replaces the old Enterprise Edition, Advanced replaces Unlimited, and Max replaces Agentforce 1. Core includes Premier Success support, Slack Business+, Tableau Next and Flex Credits for AI. That is a meaningful bundle for teams that would have bought those pieces anyway.

Hidden costs to plan for

  • Onboarding and implementation. HubSpot requires a $1,500 one time fee for Sales Hub Professional, and a larger one for Enterprise. Salesforce implementation is usually done by a partner on a quote basis.
  • AI usage. HubSpot includes monthly credits by plan and sells more at $0.010 per credit. Unused credits do not roll over. Salesforce includes Flex Credits from Core up, and sells Agentforce for Sales from $125 per user per month as an add on.
  • Admin time. A Salesforce org of 25 users usually needs someone who owns it. That may be a part time admin, a consultant or a full hire.
  • API access. Salesforce lists API access as an extra $25 per user per month on a lower suite. Check this if you plan to connect custom tools.
  • Promotional pricing. Confirm what you will pay at renewal, especially on HubSpot Starter.

Ease of use

This is the clearest difference between the two, and it is why HubSpot wins so many small business deals.

HubSpot: built to be self serve

HubSpot feels like a modern web app. The navigation is consistent, records look the same across objects, and most settings are explained in plain language. A founder can import contacts, set up a pipeline, connect an inbox and start logging emails in an afternoon. HubSpot pitches its Starter plan as something you can set up in minutes, and for basic use that is fair.

Automation is visual and forgiving. You can build a workflow by clicking through triggers and actions without knowing any code. That makes it realistic for a marketer or sales manager to own the CRM without a specialist.

The downside is that HubSpot's simplicity comes with guardrails. When you want something unusual, like a very specific approval process or a complex data model, you may hit a wall or need a workaround.

Salesforce: powerful, but you need a driver

Salesforce has improved a lot. The Lightning interface is cleaner than the old Salesforce many people remember, and the Starter Suite has guided setup aimed at small businesses. But once you move past the basics, Salesforce assumes you have an admin.

Settings are deep. Permissions, profiles, page layouts, record types and flows all interact. That is exactly what makes it powerful, and exactly what makes it easy to misconfigure. Most midsize teams on Salesforce either train an internal admin through Trailhead or hire a partner.

If nobody on your team wants to own the CRM, that is a strong signal to choose HubSpot. If you already have a Salesforce admin, most of the ease of use gap goes away.

HubSpot wins on how easy integrations are to install. Most marketplace apps connect in a few clicks, and HubSpot's own tools need no integration at all because they share one CRM. If your stack is mostly SaaS tools like Stripe, Zoom, Google Ads and a few enrichment apps, HubSpot is simpler.

Salesforce wins on depth. If you need to connect an ERP, a data warehouse, a partner portal or an on premises system, Salesforce has more mature options. It owns MuleSoft for integration and Tableau for analytics, and its partner network is huge. Salesforce itself points buyers to MuleSoft, partners or AgentExchange apps when a native connector doesn't exist.

Support

Support quality depends heavily on which plan you buy, so check this before you sign.

HubSpot support

  • Free: HubSpot Community, HubSpot Academy and the knowledge base.
  • Starter: adds email and chat support.
  • Professional and Enterprise: email and phone support.
  • Languages: support in several languages, including English, French, German, Japanese and Portuguese.

HubSpot Academy is a real strength. The courses are free, practical and good for training new hires on both the tool and the underlying marketing or sales skill.

Salesforce support

  • Standard Success Plan: included with all licenses. Mostly self guided resources.
  • Premier Success Plan: included with Core, Advanced and Max. Available as an add on for Pro Suite. Adds expert guidance and faster support.
  • Signature Success Plan: a proactive, personalized tier. Pricing is through your account executive.

Salesforce also has Trailhead, its free learning platform, plus one of the largest consultant and admin communities in software. If you get stuck, someone has almost always solved your problem before.

Our take: HubSpot support is easier to reach on lower tiers. Salesforce support is stronger at the top, especially now that Premier Success comes bundled with Core and above.

Which one should you choose?

Here is how we would decide in the most common buying situations.

You are a small business or startup with under 10 users

Choose HubSpot. Start on the free plan, then move to Starter when you need automation and branding removed. You will be productive fast and won't need a specialist. If you only need a simple sales pipeline and nothing else, also look at our Pipedrive review. It is a strong, cheaper alternative for pure sales teams.

Your marketing team runs inbound and wants sales alignment

Choose HubSpot. This is HubSpot's home ground. Forms, emails, landing pages and lead handoff all live in the same system, so marketing and sales see the same truth. If you are weighing HubSpot against a more automation focused tool, read our ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot comparison.

You have 25 to 100 sellers and a growing RevOps function

It depends on complexity. If your sales motion is fairly standard (inbound leads, one product line, one region), HubSpot Professional or Enterprise will cost less and be easier to run. If you sell multiple products across regions, need territory management or approvals, or report to a board that wants detailed forecasts, Salesforce Pro Suite or Core is the safer long term bet.

You are an enterprise or a regulated business

Choose Salesforce. Advanced and Max add the security suite, more AI capacity and expanded data capacity. The partner ecosystem and governance tools are hard to match.

You already have a Salesforce admin or partner

Lean Salesforce. The biggest downside of Salesforce is the setup and upkeep. If you already have that covered, you get the power without much of the pain.

Nobody wants to own the CRM

Choose HubSpot. An unmanaged Salesforce org tends to get messy. HubSpot is far more forgiving when the CRM is a side job for a busy manager.

Frequently asked questions

Is HubSpot cheaper than Salesforce?

On list price, yes, at every comparable tier. HubSpot Sales Hub Professional is $90 per seat per month billed annually, while Salesforce Pro Suite is $100 per user per month billed annually. HubSpot does add a required $1,500 one time onboarding fee on Professional, so year one costs can be close for small teams.

Is HubSpot easier to use than Salesforce?

Yes. HubSpot is designed for self serve setup and most small teams can run it without a specialist. Salesforce is more powerful but usually needs an admin or an implementation partner once you go beyond the basics.

Do HubSpot and Salesforce have free plans?

Both do. HubSpot's free tools work for up to 2 users and up to 1,000 contacts with no time limit. Salesforce Free Suite includes 2 user licenses. Salesforce also offers a 30 day free trial of its paid editions.

Can a small business use Salesforce?

Yes. Salesforce Starter Suite costs $25 per user per month and bundles sales, service and email marketing tools. It is a solid option, but most small businesses will find HubSpot faster to set up and easier to grow into.

Which is better for marketing teams?

HubSpot. Its marketing tools sit on the same CRM as sales, so forms, emails, landing pages and deal data connect without integration work. Salesforce includes email marketing in its suites, but advanced marketing automation is a separate product.

When should I switch from HubSpot to Salesforce?

Consider it when your sales process outgrows HubSpot's guardrails. Common signs are complex approvals, territory management, multiple product lines across regions and a need for very custom data models. Many growing teams stay on HubSpot Enterprise instead, so test both first.

What happened to Salesforce Enterprise and Unlimited editions?

Salesforce says Core replaces Enterprise Edition, Advanced replaces Unlimited Edition, and Max replaces Agentforce 1 Edition. Core is listed at $195, Advanced at $395 and Max at $550 per user per month, billed annually.

Do HubSpot and Salesforce include AI?

Yes. HubSpot includes monthly HubSpot Credits for its AI agents and features, from 500 on Starter to 5,000 on Enterprise. Salesforce includes Flex Credits for Agentforce from Core upward, and sells Agentforce for Sales as an add on from $125 per user per month.

Final recommendation

For most small and midsize teams, HubSpot is the better choice. It is easier to set up, cheaper at comparable tiers, and it puts marketing and sales on the same database without extra work. Start free, move to Starter, and upgrade to Professional when you need sequences, forecasting and custom reporting.

Salesforce is the better choice when your sales process is complex, your team is large, or you already have admin support. It costs more and takes more effort to run, but nothing else gives you the same freedom to build the CRM around your business.

Still unsure? Try both. HubSpot's free plan has no time limit, and Salesforce offers a 30 day free trial. Load your own data, build one real pipeline in each, and see which one your team opens without being asked.
