Salesforce Review (2026): Features, Pricing, Pros and Cons

Salesforce is the best CRM for large or complex sales teams that need a platform they can shape around almost any process. It is powerful and it scales. It also costs more and needs someone to run it.

Is Salesforce worth it? Yes, if you run a midsize or large sales team with a process that doesn't fit neatly into a simple pipeline. Salesforce gives you deeper customization, forecasting and governance than any other CRM we review, and it scales from a few users to thousands. The trade off is cost and complexity. Paid suites start at $25 per user per month, but the editions most growing teams end up on run from $195 to $550 per user per month, billed annually. Most teams also need an admin or a partner. We score Salesforce 8.9 out of 10.

What is Salesforce?

Salesforce is a cloud CRM and business platform. Most people mean Sales Cloud when they say "Salesforce". That is the product sales teams use to manage leads, accounts, contacts, opportunities and forecasts.

Around Sales Cloud sit many other products. Service Cloud handles support. Marketing Cloud handles campaigns and journeys. Data 360, which Salesforce used to call Data Cloud, unifies customer data. Agentforce is the AI agent layer. Slack and Tableau, both owned by Salesforce, come bundled with the higher editions. AppExchange is the marketplace where partners sell apps that plug into all of it.

The key thing to understand is that Salesforce is a platform first and an app second. Almost everything can be changed. You can add custom objects, build your own apps, design approval chains, set up territory rules and control exactly who sees which field. That flexibility is why big companies standardize on it. It is also why a badly managed Salesforce org can get messy fast.

Salesforce now sells its CRM in two layers. For smaller teams there are suites: Free Suite, Starter Suite and Pro Suite. For larger teams there are Sales Cloud editions: Core, Advanced and Max. In 2026 those editions replaced the old Enterprise, Unlimited and Agentforce 1 names, so older guides and quotes may not match what you see today.

Key features

Below we cover each part of the platform, what it does well and where limits sit by plan. Plan details come from Salesforce's official pricing and comparison pages, checked November 2026.

Sales Cloud

Sales Cloud is the core CRM. Every plan includes lead, account, contact and opportunity management, a customizable sales process, tasks, an activity feed, email integration with Gmail or Outlook and the Salesforce mobile app.

Where it pulls ahead of simpler CRMs is in how much you can shape it. Profiles, roles and permissions control access down to the field. Record types let you run different processes for different products or regions. The Lightning App Builder lets admins design pages without code. Flow Builder automates routine work, and approval processes handle discounts and contract sign off.

Forecasting is a real strength. Pro Suite adds quoting and forecasting. From Core up you get advanced forecast and pipeline management, opportunity scoring, pipeline inspection, deal insights, opportunity splits and sales territories. For a sales leader who reports to a board, that matters.

  • Lead assignment and routing, duplicate blocking and web to lead capture
  • Products, price books, quotes, contracts and orders
  • Customizable reports and dashboards, with Tableau Next on higher editions
  • Developer sandboxes, plus a partial sandbox included from Core and a full sandbox included on Advanced and Max
  • Slack included: Slack Free on the suites, Slack Business+ on Core and Advanced, Slack Enterprise+ on Max

Starter Suite and Pro Suite

The suites are Salesforce's answer to the "too big for small businesses" complaint. They bundle sales, service, marketing and commerce tools into one simpler package with guided setup.

Free Suite costs nothing and includes 2 user licenses. It needs no contract or credit card. You get basic lead and account tracking, simple email marketing and service case management. Salesforce says you only pay for email sends beyond the monthly limit, or by upgrading.

Starter Suite costs $25 per user per month, billed monthly or annually. It covers lead, account, contact and opportunity management, built in sales flows and lead routing, AI that syncs emails, events and contacts, and dynamic email marketing with analytics. Salesforce also includes Slack at no extra cost. You can trial it free for 30 days with up to 10 users.

Pro Suite costs $100 per user per month, billed annually. It adds more customization and automation, sales quoting and forecasting, and access to AgentExchange. Premier Support is available as an add on.

Our view: Starter Suite is a credible small business CRM now. It is not as easy as HubSpot or Pipedrive, but it gives you a path to the full platform without migrating later. Watch the limits. The comparison table shows lower plans capped on things like flows and custom apps, so check the plan comparison before you build automation you can't run.

This is where most midsize and enterprise teams land. Salesforce says Core replaces Enterprise Edition, Advanced replaces Unlimited Edition and Max replaces Agentforce 1 Edition. Each new edition bundles more than the old one did.

Advanced is for teams with serious security and compliance needs. The security suite is the clear difference. Max is for companies that want AI across the whole sales team without metering each employee's agent use, plus incentive compensation and territory planning tools.

Agentforce AI

Agentforce is Salesforce's AI agent platform. It covers predictive, generative and agentic AI across sales, service and other clouds. For sales, Salesforce lists agents and skills for prospecting, lead nurture, pipeline management, sales emails, account research and employee agent actions, along with call summaries, sales signals and lead scoring.

You can buy Agentforce in three ways:

  • Flex Credits. A consumption model. Each action an agent takes draws from a pool of credits. Salesforce says a standard Agentforce action uses 20 Flex Credits and an Agentforce Voice action uses 30. Its own examples price a single action at $0.10.
  • Conversations. Flat pricing per conversation, aimed at customer facing agents. You can't run Conversations and Flex Credits in the same org.
  • Per user add ons. Agentforce add ons for Sales, Service and Field Service are listed at $125 per user per month with unmetered employee use. Industries add ons are $150. Max includes the add on.

There is also a standalone Agentforce Coworker license at $50 per user per month, which Salesforce describes as an AI teammate that searches across your enterprise data under your existing security rules.

Our take: Agentforce is the most ambitious CRM AI we review, and it benefits from sitting on your real Salesforce data and permissions. But it adds a new cost line. Unused Flex Credits do not roll over into the next term. Use Salesforce's Digital Wallet to watch consumption and set alerts before you roll agents out widely.

AppExchange

AppExchange is Salesforce's marketplace. Salesforce describes it as home to more than 7,000 prebuilt apps and certified consulting partners. It now sits alongside AgentExchange, a marketplace for agents and AI tools, which Pro Suite and above can access.

This ecosystem is a big part of why Salesforce scores so well on integrations. Need electronic signatures, CPQ, data enrichment, document generation or an ERP connector? Someone has almost certainly built it. For systems without a ready made connector, such as on premises data sources, Salesforce points buyers to MuleSoft, its integration platform, or to partners.

One cost to know: Salesforce's comparison table lists Web Services API access at an extra $25 per user per month on its suite tier. Core includes APIs. If you plan to connect custom tools, check this before you buy a suite.

Data Cloud (now Data 360)

Data 360 is Salesforce's customer data platform, previously sold as Data Cloud. It pulls data in from many sources, cleans it, matches and merges records into unified customer profiles, then lets you build segments and send them to other channels. It also feeds Agentforce, so agents can act on fuller customer context.

Salesforce prices Data 360 on usage. Bringing data in is free. You pay when you use the data: preparing it, unifying profiles, segmenting, activating, querying and streaming. There are three published models:

ModelListed priceGood for

Salesforce customers can also provision Data 360 free with a limited amount of storage and credits, and Pro Suite lists limited Data Cloud features. For most sales teams, that free allowance is enough to start. Marketing teams building real time personalization will need a proper budget, so use Salesforce's pricing calculator.

Add ons that change the bill

The headline seat price is rarely the final number. These are the add ons Salesforce lists on its Sales Cloud pricing page:

  • Agentforce for Sales: from $125 per user per month (included in Max).
  • Sales Programs: from $100 per user per month (included in Advanced and Max).
  • Revenue Intelligence: from $220 per user per month.
  • Revenue Cloud: from $200 per user per month, for quote to cash.
  • Partner Relationship Management: from $10 per login per month.
  • Web Services API: an extra $25 per user per month on the suite tier.
  • Premier Success: an add on for Pro Suite, included from Core up. Signature Success is quoted by your account executive.

Then there is implementation. Salesforce doesn't list an onboarding fee the way HubSpot does, but most teams moving to Core or above hire a certified partner, and that work is quote based. Budget for it.

Support plans

  • Standard Success Plan: included with every license. Self guided resources.
  • Premier Success Plan: expert guidance and faster support. Included with Core, Advanced and Max.
  • Signature Success Plan: a proactive, personalized tier. Pricing through your account executive.

Trailhead, Salesforce's free learning platform, is excellent. Combined with one of the biggest admin and consultant communities in software, you rarely face a problem nobody has solved before.

Pros

  • Unmatched flexibility. Custom objects, record types, permissions and approvals let you model almost any sales process.
  • Built to scale. It handles a handful of users or thousands without switching platforms.
  • Deep forecasting and pipeline tools. Pipeline inspection, opportunity scoring, territories and splits from Core up.
  • Strong bundles from Core. Premier Success, Slack Business+, Tableau Next, Data 360 and Flex Credits come included.
  • The biggest ecosystem in CRM. AppExchange, AgentExchange, MuleSoft, partners and certified admins.
  • Serious security and governance. Advanced adds Backup and Recover, Archive, Data Detect and Security Center.
  • A real entry path. Free Suite and Starter Suite let small teams start without a big contract.

Cons

  • Expensive past the suites. Core starts at $195 per user per month, nearly double Pro Suite.
  • Add ons stack up fast. AI, revenue intelligence, API access on suites and marketing automation all cost extra.
  • Needs an admin. Beyond the suites, most teams need a trained admin or a partner to set up and maintain it.
  • Annual contracts. Pro Suite and above are billed annually, so mistakes are expensive.
  • Marketing automation is separate. Marketing Cloud is priced per org from $1,500 a month.
  • AI costs are harder to predict. Flex Credits are consumed per action and unused credits do not roll over.
  • Constant renaming. Edition and product names changed in 2026, so older guides and quotes may not match.

Who Salesforce is best for

Salesforce is a great fit if you are

  • A midsize or enterprise sales organization with multiple teams, regions or product lines.
  • A company with complex deals that need approvals, quoting, contracts and territory rules.
  • A regulated or security focused business that needs backup, archiving and data detection tools.
  • A team with an admin or partner already in place. That removes most of the pain.
  • A fast growing startup that expects to need deep customization in a year or two and wants to avoid a migration later. Starter Suite is a sensible first step.

Who should skip Salesforce

  • Small sales teams that just need a pipeline. Pipedrive is simpler and cheaper.
  • Marketing led small businesses. HubSpot puts marketing and sales on one record without a separate marketing product.
  • Email first businesses. ActiveCampaign gives you strong automation for far less than Marketing Cloud.
  • Teams where nobody wants to own the CRM. An unmanaged Salesforce org gets messy quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Is Salesforce worth it in 2026?

For midsize and large sales teams with complex processes, yes. Nothing else can be shaped as deeply around how you sell. For small teams with a simple pipeline, it is often more tool than you need. Start with Starter Suite or the Free Suite and only move up when you have a clear reason.

How much does Salesforce cost per user?

Salesforce lists a Free Suite at $0, Starter Suite at $25 per user per month, Pro Suite at $100 per user per month billed annually, and the Sales Cloud editions Core at $195, Advanced at $395 and Max at $550 per user per month billed annually. Pricing checked November 2026.

Does Salesforce have a free plan?

Yes. The Free Suite includes 2 user licenses with no contract and no credit card. It covers basic sales, service cases and simple email marketing. Salesforce also offers a 30 day free trial of its Sales Cloud editions, and a 30 day Starter Suite trial for up to 10 users.

What happened to Salesforce Enterprise and Unlimited editions?

Salesforce says Core replaces Enterprise Edition, Advanced replaces Unlimited Edition and Max replaces Agentforce 1 Edition. The new editions bundle Premier Success support, Slack, Tableau Next, Data 360 and Agentforce capacity through Flex Credits.

What is Agentforce and how is it priced?

Agentforce is Salesforce's AI agent platform. You can pay per use with Flex Credits, where a standard Agentforce action uses 20 credits, or buy per user add ons such as Agentforce for Sales from $125 per user per month. Core, Advanced and Max include a pool of Flex Credits, and Max includes the Agentforce add on.

Is Salesforce hard to use?

The everyday interface is fine for sales reps. The hard part is setup and upkeep. Permissions, page layouts, record types and flows all interact, so most teams beyond the suites need a trained admin or an implementation partner.

Is Salesforce better than HubSpot?

Salesforce is better for complex sales organizations that need custom data models, territories, approvals and enterprise governance. HubSpot is better for teams that want fast setup and marketing tools on the same record without an admin. Our HubSpot vs Salesforce comparison covers the trade offs.

Can I pay for Salesforce monthly?

Starter Suite can be billed monthly or annually. Pro Suite and the Core, Advanced and Max editions are listed as billed annually, and Salesforce says most of its products use annual contracts paid in advance. Plan on a yearly commitment.

Final verdict

Salesforce earns an 8.9 out of 10. It is the most powerful and most scalable CRM we review. If your sales process is complex, your team is large or your compliance needs are serious, it is the safe long term choice.

The catch is effort and money. Past the suites, you pay a premium per seat, add ons pile up, and someone has to own the system. If that person doesn't exist, you will feel it.

Our advice is simple. Small teams should start on Free Suite or Starter Suite and see if the team adopts it. Growing teams should price Core at their year two headcount, with partner fees and any AI add ons included. If the number still makes sense, Salesforce will not hold you back.
