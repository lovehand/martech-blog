Is HubSpot worth it? Yes, for most growing businesses. HubSpot gives you a genuinely useful free CRM, a cheap Starter plan that covers marketing, sales and service, and Professional plans that are among the easiest automation platforms to run without a full time admin. The downside is cost. Prices jump sharply at Professional, onboarding fees are mandatory there, and marketing contacts and seats add up fast. We score HubSpot 8.6 out of 10.

What is HubSpot?

HubSpot is a customer platform. At its center is the Smart CRM, a shared database for your contacts, companies, deals and tickets. On top of that sit separate products HubSpot calls Hubs. You can buy one Hub, a few, or all of them.

The main Hubs are Marketing Hub, Sales Hub, Service Hub and Content Hub. There is also Data Hub for syncing and cleaning data, and HubSpot now sells a Revenue Hub alongside Sales Hub for teams that want quoting and payments in the same place. Across all of them runs Breeze, HubSpot's AI layer, which includes an in app assistant and a growing set of AI agents.

The big idea is simple. Your marketing, sales and support teams all look at the same customer record. A sales rep can see which emails a lead opened. A support agent can see the deal history. A marketer can build a list based on tickets or deal stage. That shared view is the real reason teams choose HubSpot over a stack of cheaper point tools.

HubSpot has a reputation for being easy to use, and that is mostly earned. The flip side is that the platform is now huge. There are a lot of features, a lot of plan limits and a pricing model that mixes seats, contacts and credits. Most of this review is about helping you work out which parts you actually need.

Key features

Below we cover each part of the platform, what it does well and where the limits sit by plan. Limits come from HubSpot's official pricing pages, checked November 2026.

Smart CRM

The Smart CRM is the foundation. It stores contacts, companies, deals, tickets and activity history, and it is included with every HubSpot account, free or paid. HubSpot describes Sales Hub as the tools built on top of it, with the CRM as the system of record.

What we like is how little setup it needs. You can import a spreadsheet, connect your inbox and start logging emails and meetings in an afternoon. Records show a clean timeline of every touchpoint, and filters and views are easy to build without training.

The free version has real limits now. HubSpot's free tools allow up to 1,000 contacts and 1,000 records across other standard object types per account, for up to 2 users. That is fine for testing or a very small business. Growing teams will hit it quickly.

  • Contact, company, deal and ticket records with a full activity timeline
  • Inbox connection, email tracking and logging
  • Meeting scheduling links (1 personal link on free, up to 1,000 on paid plans)
  • Reporting dashboards: 10 on free, 30 on Starter, 75 on Professional and 100 on Enterprise
  • Free mobile app

Marketing Hub

Marketing Hub is HubSpot's best known product. It covers email marketing, forms, landing pages, ads, social media, automation, lead scoring and reporting.

On the free tools you get 2,000 email sends a month with HubSpot branding and a single automated action. Starter removes branding, lets you authenticate your own sending domain and raises the send limit to 5 times your marketing contact tier each month, with up to 10 automated actions per email automation.

Professional is where Marketing Hub becomes a real automation platform. You get omnichannel workflows (up to 300), social publishing, custom reporting, A/B testing, smart content and lead scoring with up to 5 scores. The email send limit rises to 10 times your contact tier. Enterprise adds up to 1,000 workflows, behavioral events, multi touch revenue attribution, customer journey analytics, up to 50 lead scores and a 20 times send limit.

The email editor and workflow builder are where HubSpot earns its ease of use score. Building a nurture sequence with branches is visual and readable, and your sales team sees the result inside the same record. For most marketing teams, Professional covers everything they will realistically use.

One newer addition worth knowing about is HubSpot AEO, a tool that tracks how your brand appears in AI answer engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. HubSpot listed it at $45 a month billed annually for 25 prompts tracked daily across 3 engines.

Sales Hub

Sales Hub adds the tools reps use every day: pipelines, sequences, calling, playbooks, forecasting and conversation intelligence.

Starter gives you up to 15 pipelines, 500 calling minutes, 1 HubSpot provided phone number and up to 50 workflows with restricted triggers. It is a solid upgrade from the free CRM for a small team.

Professional is the plan most growing sales teams end up on. It adds sequences (500 email sends per user per day), up to 300 fully customizable workflows, 100 pipelines, 3,000 calling minutes, call transcription, forecasting, playbooks and lead scoring. Enterprise raises those limits further: 350 pipelines, 12,000 calling minutes, 1,000 workflows, 1,000 sequence sends per user per day and forecasting across team hierarchies.

In our evaluation, Sales Hub scores well because reps actually adopt it. The interface is less intimidating than Salesforce, and marketing context sits right on the deal. Where it falls behind is deep customization for unusual sales processes, which is still Salesforce territory.

Service Hub

Service Hub is HubSpot's help desk and customer success product. It includes ticketing, a shared help desk workspace, live chat, knowledge base, customer feedback surveys and SLAs.

Professional is where it becomes a full help desk. You get a knowledge base with up to 2,000 articles, 500 macros, skill based routing, SLAs, customer success workspace with health scores, and NPS, CES and CSAT surveys. Enterprise adds up to 25 knowledge bases with 10,000 total articles, 1,500 macros and conditional SLAs.

The standout is the Customer Agent, an AI agent that answers common questions across chat, email, forms and calling and hands off to a human when it needs to. At the time of checking, HubSpot was offering 14 days of unlimited Customer Agent use when you buy at least one Service Hub Professional or Enterprise seat. After that it runs on HubSpot Credits.

If your support team already lives in a dedicated help desk with complex routing, Service Hub may feel lighter. If you want support and sales on the same record, it is a strong choice.

Content Hub

Content Hub is HubSpot's website builder and content management system. It covers website pages, landing pages, blogs, podcasts, video hosting and AI content tools.

The free tools let you publish one blog with up to 100 posts and up to 30 website pages and 30 landing pages, with HubSpot branding. Starter removes branding and raises the blog to 10,000 posts. Professional opens up to 10,000 website and landing pages, smart content, A/B and adaptive testing, SEO recommendations with site auditing, memberships and HubDB for dynamic pages. Enterprise adds multisite support and more scale.

AI features include an image generator, AI translation and content remix, which can repurpose one piece into several formats (up to 20 a day on Professional and 50 on Enterprise).

Content Hub makes the most sense if you want your website, forms and CRM in one system with no plugins to maintain. If you already run a well built site on another platform, you can use HubSpot forms and tracking without moving your website.

AI and Breeze features

Breeze is HubSpot's AI layer. It has two parts you will notice.

Breeze Assistant is the in app helper. It answers questions about your CRM data, drafts emails and summarizes records. Agents are AI tools that do work on their own. HubSpot lists Customer Agent, Data Agent, Prospecting Agent, Campaign Agent, Content Agent and Nurture Agent, plus Agent Builder for creating custom agents. Some of these are labeled beta.

  • Customer Agent answers customer questions across channels and escalates when needed.
  • Data Agent researches contacts and companies using your CRM and the web, and keeps records up to date.
  • Prospecting Agent watches accounts for buying signals and drafts outreach.
  • Agent Builder lets you build custom agents. Starter gets simple automation. Professional and Enterprise add advanced automation and event based triggers.

Agents and some AI features run on HubSpot Credits. Paid plans include a monthly allowance: 500 credits on Starter, 3,000 on Professional and 5,000 on Enterprise. Credits reset each month and do not roll over. Extra credits cost $0.010 each, so $10 per 1,000 credits, either in packs or pay as you go. If you buy several Hubs, included credits are not added together. You get the allowance of your highest tier.

Our take: the AI is useful, especially Customer Agent and Data Agent, and it benefits from sitting on your real CRM data. But credits make costs less predictable. Set spending limits in the account before you switch agents on.

Free tools

HubSpot's free tools cost nothing, need no credit card and have no time limit. You get the Smart CRM plus light versions of marketing, sales, service and content tools for up to 2 users.

The limits to know: 1,000 contacts, 1,000 records across other standard objects, 2,000 marketing email sends a month, one email automation action, one personal meetings link, and HubSpot branding on emails, forms, chat and pages. Support is self serve through HubSpot Community, Academy and the knowledge base.

Free is a great way to test HubSpot properly before paying. It is not a long term home for a business with a growing list.

Starter

Starter is now sold as one Starter plan that covers essential marketing, sales, service, content and data tools. HubSpot priced it per seat. When we checked, the pricing pages showed $7 per seat per month as a limited time offer for new customers (marketed as saving up to 65%), next to a standard price of $20 per seat per month. HubSpot does not make the offer's duration clear on the page, so assume renewal at the standard rate and confirm terms at checkout.

Starter removes HubSpot branding, raises limits and adds email and chat support. It also adds automation, payments, electronic signatures, multi currency support, 500 calling minutes, a dedicated phone number and 500 HubSpot Credits a month. Marketing Hub Starter includes 1,000 marketing contacts. There is no required onboarding fee on Starter.

For a team of 1 to 5 people, Starter is one of the best value plans in the CRM market.

Professional

Professional is the big jump, and the plan where HubSpot's reputation is built.

  • Marketing Hub Professional: $800 a month, including 3 core seats and 2,000 marketing contacts. Additional core seats start at $45 a month. Includes 3,000 HubSpot Credits. Required one time onboarding of $3,000.
  • Sales Hub Professional: $90 per seat per month billed annually, or $100 paid monthly. Includes 3,000 credits. Required one time onboarding of $1,500.
  • Service Hub Professional: $90 per seat per month billed annually, or $100 paid monthly. Required one time onboarding of $1,500.
  • Content Hub Professional: $450 a month, with additional core seats starting at $45 a month.

Support moves up too. Professional and Enterprise plans include email and phone support.

Enterprise

  • Marketing Hub Enterprise: $3,600 a month, including 5 core seats and 10,000 marketing contacts. HubSpot's pricing guide lists additional seats at $75 a month and onboarding at $7,000.
  • Sales Hub Enterprise: $150 per seat per month. HubSpot's regional pricing page lists required Enterprise onboarding at $3,500.
  • Service Hub Enterprise: $150 per seat per month.
  • Content Hub Enterprise: $1,500 a month.

Enterprise plans include 5,000 HubSpot Credits a month. Most Enterprise deals go through HubSpot sales, so expect a quote and room to negotiate on multi year or multi Hub contracts.

How seats work

HubSpot has three kinds of seats. Understanding them saves real money.

  • Core Seats give full access to your plan's features. Marketing Hub and Content Hub Professional and Enterprise include a set number, and you pay for more.
  • Sales Seats and Service Seats are how Sales Hub and Service Hub Professional and Enterprise are priced. You pay per user who needs those tools.
  • View only Seats are free. Give them to managers and stakeholders who only need to look at records and reports.

One rule catches people out. HubSpot prices all Core Seats in an account at the level of your highest subscription. If you have Marketing Hub Professional and Sales Hub Enterprise, your core seats are priced at the Enterprise level.

Onboarding fees

Professional and Enterprise plans of Marketing Hub, Sales Hub and Service Hub carry a required one time onboarding fee. It is charged per Hub. HubSpot's guide says onboarding covers implementation guidance, data migration help and training with a HubSpot specialist. It does not mean HubSpot builds your account for you.

Our advice: treat onboarding as part of the first year cost, not a surprise. If you work with a HubSpot partner agency, ask whether they can handle onboarding as part of their work, as arrangements vary.

Marketing contact tier pricing

Marketing Hub is priced on marketing contacts, the contacts you can send marketing emails and ads to. Starter includes 1,000, Professional 2,000 and Enterprise 10,000. You can store other contacts as non marketing contacts for free, up to a limit of 15 million overall contacts, but you cannot target them with marketing emails or ads.

Go over your tier and you move to the next one. HubSpot's own guide gives an example: moving a Professional account from 2,000 to the 5,000 contact tier adds roughly $250 a month. After the first 2,000, Professional contacts are sold in predefined tiers, while Enterprise sells them in larger blocks of 10,000. Once you pick a tier, you cannot downgrade until your contract renews. HubSpot updates marketing contact status once a month, so cleaning your list regularly keeps your bill down.

Exact tier prices depend on your plan and volume, so use the calculator on the Marketing Hub pricing page for your numbers.

Pros

  • Everything shares one record. Marketing, sales and service data sit on the same contact, which makes handoffs and reporting much easier.
  • A real free plan. You can test the CRM properly with no card and no time limit.
  • Starter is great value. One low per seat price covers tools from every Hub.
  • Easy to learn. New users become productive quickly, and admins don't need to be developers.
  • Strong automation at Professional. Visual workflows, sequences and lead scoring that marketing and sales teams can run themselves.
  • Huge ecosystem. The App Marketplace, a mature API, HubSpot Academy and a big partner network.
  • Useful AI on your own data. Agents like Customer Agent and Data Agent work from your CRM, not generic prompts.

Cons

  • The price jump to Professional is steep. Marketing Hub goes from a per seat Starter price to $800 a month, plus onboarding.
  • Mandatory onboarding fees. $1,500 to $7,000 per Hub on Professional and Enterprise.
  • Contact based pricing punishes big lists. Marketing contact tiers rise as your database grows, and you can't downgrade mid contract.
  • Pricing is complicated. Seats, contacts, credits and Hub levels all interact. Budgeting takes work.
  • The free plan is tighter than it used to be. 1,000 contacts and 2 users won't last a growing business long.
  • AI credits add variable cost. Included credits reset monthly and do not roll over.
  • Less flexible than Salesforce for very complex or unusual sales processes.

Who HubSpot is best for

HubSpot is a great fit if you are

  • A small business or startup that wants a free or low cost CRM you won't outgrow in a year.
  • A B2B company with an inbound marketing focus that needs email, forms, landing pages and lead scoring tied to sales.
  • A midsize team aligning marketing, sales and service that is tired of syncing data between separate tools.
  • A team without a dedicated CRM admin. HubSpot is far easier to run than heavier enterprise platforms.
  • An agency managing campaigns and websites for clients, thanks to the large partner ecosystem.

Who should skip HubSpot

  • Sales teams that only need a pipeline. A focused CRM like Pipedrive is cheaper and simpler.
  • Email first businesses on a budget, such as ecommerce or creators with big lists. ActiveCampaign usually costs less per contact.
  • Large enterprises with complex processes and an admin team. Salesforce offers more customization.
  • Very cost sensitive teams that need many users. Zoho CRM is hard to beat on price.

Salesforce

Salesforce is the most customizable CRM on the market. Its pricing page lists a Free Suite, Starter Suite at $25 per user per month, Pro Suite at $100 per user per month billed annually, and Sales Cloud editions called Core at $195, Advanced at $395 and Max at $550 per user per month billed annually.

Choose Salesforce if you have complex territories, custom objects everywhere and an admin team to manage it. Choose HubSpot if you want faster setup and marketing built in. Read our Salesforce review and our HubSpot vs Salesforce comparison.

Pipedrive

Pipedrive is a sales first CRM built around a visual pipeline. Its plans start at $14 per seat per month billed annually ($168 per seat per year), with the top plan at $79 per seat per month billed annually. It offers a 14 day free trial, 500+ integrations and add ons for lead capture and email campaigns.

Pick Pipedrive if your main need is managing deals and you don't need marketing automation. Read our Pipedrive review.

ActiveCampaign

ActiveCampaign is an email marketing and automation platform with a CRM. ActiveCampaign says its Starter plan starts at $15 a month for 1,000 contacts. Higher plans (Plus, Pro and Enterprise) add landing pages, more seats, predictive sending and attribution. Pipelines and sales engagement are paid add ons.

Pick ActiveCampaign if email automation is your main channel and you want lower cost per contact than HubSpot. Read our ActiveCampaign review and ActiveCampaign vs HubSpot.

Zoho CRM

Zoho CRM is a flexible, budget friendly CRM. It is free forever for up to 3 users, with paid Standard, Professional, Enterprise and Ultimate editions priced per user. Zoho also sells Zoho One, a bundle of 50+ apps starting at $30 per user per month. Paid edition prices vary by region and billing term, so check the Zoho CRM pricing page.

Pick Zoho if you need lots of users on a tight budget and are happy trading some polish for price. More options are in our best tools hub and comparisons hub.

Frequently asked questions

Is HubSpot worth it in 2026?

For most small and midsize teams that want marketing, sales and service in one place, yes. The free tools and Starter plan are excellent value. Professional is worth it once you will actually use automation, reporting and sequences, but budget for the onboarding fee and extra seats before you commit.

How much does HubSpot cost per month?

HubSpot's free tools cost nothing for up to 2 users. Starter showed $7 per seat per month as a limited time new customer offer, against a standard $20 per seat per month. Marketing Hub Professional starts at $800 per month with 3 core seats, and Sales Hub or Service Hub Professional cost $90 per seat per month billed annually. Pricing checked November 2026.

Is HubSpot CRM really free?

Yes. The free tools include the Smart CRM with no time limit for up to 2 users. The catch is the limits: 1,000 contacts, 1,000 records across other standard objects, 2,000 marketing email sends a month and HubSpot branding on emails, forms and chat.

Does HubSpot charge an onboarding fee?

Yes, on Professional and Enterprise. HubSpot lists a one time fee of $3,000 for Marketing Hub Professional and $1,500 for Sales Hub or Service Hub Professional. HubSpot's own pricing guide lists $7,000 for Marketing Hub Enterprise, and HubSpot's regional pricing page shows $3,500 for Sales Hub Enterprise. Starter has no required onboarding.

What are HubSpot marketing contacts?

Marketing contacts are the contacts you can send marketing emails and ads to. They are what you pay for in Marketing Hub. Starter includes 1,000, Professional 2,000 and Enterprise 10,000. You can store other contacts as non marketing contacts for free, but you cannot email them with marketing campaigns.

What is HubSpot Breeze?

Breeze is the name HubSpot uses for its AI layer. It covers Breeze Assistant inside the app plus a set of AI agents, such as Customer Agent, Data Agent and Prospecting Agent, that run on HubSpot Credits. Paid plans include a monthly allowance of credits and you can buy more.

Is HubSpot better than Salesforce?

HubSpot is easier to set up and better for teams that want marketing and sales together without hiring an admin. Salesforce is more customizable and suits large or complex sales organizations. Our HubSpot vs Salesforce comparison goes through the trade offs in detail.

Can I cancel HubSpot at any time?

You can turn off auto renewal, but paid plans run to the end of the term you signed up for. On annual billing that means you pay for the full year. When the term ends, your account drops back to the free tools rather than being deleted.

Final verdict

HubSpot is the CRM we recommend most often to small and midsize teams, and it earns an 8.6 out of 10. The free tools let you try it with no risk. Starter is excellent value. Professional is genuinely powerful and still easy enough to run without a full time admin.

The catch is money. Once you move to Professional, you are paying for seats, contacts, onboarding and possibly credits. That is fine if you use the automation and reporting. It is wasteful if you only need a pipeline.

Our advice is simple. Start on the free tools. Move to Starter when you hit the limits. Only upgrade to Professional when you have a clear plan for workflows, sequences and reporting, and budget the first year with onboarding included.
