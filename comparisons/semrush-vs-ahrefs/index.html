Semrush vs Ahrefs (2026): Which SEO Tool Should You Buy?

Semrush is the better pick if you want one platform for SEO, AI search, ads, content and client reporting. Ahrefs is the better pick if backlinks and fast, clean research data matter most. Here is how they compare on features, real pricing for a solo user and a small agency, data, ease of use, integrations and support.

Short answer: choose Semrush if you want one tool that covers SEO, AI search visibility, ads research, content and client reports, and you like the idea of adding toolkits as you grow. Choose Ahrefs if your work lives in backlinks, competitor research and keyword data, and you want a cleaner tool that does fewer things very well. Ahrefs is cheaper to start. Semrush gives you more breadth for the money once you use more than SEO.

Quick comparison

These are the two biggest names in SEO software. Both will help you find keywords, spy on competitors, audit your site and track rankings. Both now track how your brand shows up in AI answers too. So you can't really go wrong.

But they feel different. Semrush is a marketing platform. It started with SEO and kept adding things: ads research, local listings, social, PR, content writing and traffic analytics. Ahrefs is a research tool first. It built its name on backlink data and still feels like a tool made by SEOs for SEOs.

So the real question isn't "which is better?" It is "do I need a wide toolbox, or a sharp one?"

Where Semrush pulls ahead

Semrush is the wider tool. It's not even close. If you run SEO and also touch PPC, local listings, social or PR, Semrush has a toolkit for each one. You add them when you need them and drop them when you don't.

That matters most for agencies. One login can cover keyword research for a new client, an ads teardown of their competitor, a local listings fix and a branded monthly report. With Ahrefs you would likely need other tools for some of that.

Semrush is also generous with daily rank tracking at the entry level. You get 500 tracked keywords on the cheapest paid plan. And AI prompt tracking starts on the Starter plan, with daily checks built in.

Where Ahrefs pulls ahead

Ahrefs is the sharper research tool. Its backlink reports are the reason many SEOs still pay for it. The reports are fast, the filters are good, and its Domain Rating metric is one of the most quoted numbers in SEO.

It is also friendlier to people who own several sites. Verified projects are unlimited on every plan, including the free one. If you can prove you own a site, you can audit it and see its backlinks without using up your project limits.

And from Standard up, Ahrefs gives you unlimited credits. You can load and filter reports as much as you like. That freedom is a big deal if you spend all day digging.

What each plan really includes

The plan names don't line up neatly, so here is the plain English version.

  • Semrush SEO covers the basics: 5 websites, 500 tracked keywords, keyword and competitor research, Position Tracking, Site Audit and some AI visibility reports. It does not include a prompt tracking quota.
  • Semrush Starter adds fuller keyword and competitor tools, MCP access and AI visibility with 50 prompts tracked daily for 1 domain.
  • Semrush Pro+ moves you to 15 websites and 1,500 tracked keywords, and adds historical data, content optimization, cannibalization checks, multi location tracking and 100 daily prompts.
  • Ahrefs Starter is a light plan with limited monthly credits, 1 unverified project, 1 month of history and 50 tracked keywords. No extra users.
  • Ahrefs Lite gives 1,000 credits per user, 5 unverified projects, 6 months of history, 750 tracked keywords, API and MCP access and 150 AI prompt checks.
  • Ahrefs Standard unlocks unlimited credits, 20 unverified projects, 2 years of history, 2,000 tracked keywords, Content Explorer, Portfolios and Batch Analysis.

Hidden costs to plan for

  • Extra users. Both tools include 1 user. Semrush charges from $45 per user per month. Ahrefs charges $40 on Lite (up to 2 more), $60 on Standard (up to 5) and $80 on Advanced (up to 10).
  • Add on toolkits. Semrush sells Content, Local, Social, Advertising, AI PR and Traffic & Market as separate toolkits.
  • Standalone tools. Ahrefs sells Brand Radar from $199 per month per AI platform, custom AI prompt packages from $50 per month, Traffic Analytics reports at $99 per month and AI Content Helper at $99 per month.
  • Credits and overages. Ahrefs Starter and Lite have monthly credit limits. You can buy more on a pay as you go basis. Ahrefs says extra users and pay as you go usage can be charged automatically.
  • Refunds. Ahrefs says it does not issue refunds in general. Semrush lets you cancel, upgrade or downgrade at any time unless you signed custom terms.

Ease of use

Both tools are easier to use than they were a few years ago. But they feel very different on day one.

Ahrefs: clean and focused

Ahrefs has a tidy, simple layout. You type a domain or a keyword and you get a clear overview. The core tools are few and clearly named: Site Explorer, Keywords Explorer, Site Audit, Rank Tracker and a handful more. It's easy to find your way back to where you were.

The reports load fast and the filters are strong. Most beginners can get useful answers in their first session. The trade off is that the cheaper plans use credits. Every report you load or filter on Starter and Lite counts against your monthly limit. Some people find that stressful. On Standard and above, credits are unlimited and the worry goes away.

Semrush: bigger, with more to learn

Semrush has more tools, so it has more menus. Tools are grouped by toolkit, which helps. But the first week can feel like walking into a big hardware store. Everything you need is there. You just have to learn the aisles.

Once you know your way around, it is pleasant to use. Projects tie your site audit, rank tracking and on page ideas together. Reports are easy to share with clients or a boss who isn't an SEO. And the 7 day trial gives you time to learn the layout before you pay, as long as you don't need to export data during that week.

Our take: if you want the quickest path to clear SEO answers, Ahrefs feels lighter. If you want one place to run lots of marketing tasks, Semrush is worth the extra learning time.

The best test is free and takes 20 minutes. Pick three competitor domains you know well. Look them up in both tools. Compare the backlinks, the top keywords and the traffic estimates against what you know is true. The tool that gets your market right is the right tool for you.

Support

Support depends on whether you pay. On both tools, free users get much less help.

Semrush support

  • All users: knowledge base, blog and live or on demand webinars.
  • Free plan: help with billing, account, legal and security questions only. No product support.
  • Paid plans: personal help from the support team on any Semrush question, including product issues.
  • Enterprise: a dedicated account manager and 24/7 support.

Ahrefs support

  • Help center: a large library of guides for each tool, from Site Explorer to Site Audit.
  • Live chat and email: Ahrefs points users to live chat (with an option to talk to a person) and email support.
  • Learning content: the Ahrefs blog and its regular live sessions are some of the best free SEO training around.

Our take: it's close to a tie. Semrush gives you more structure and an account manager at the top end. Ahrefs makes it easy to reach a real person over chat. Both publish excellent free learning content, which is often faster than any ticket.

Which one should you choose?

Here is how we would decide in the most common situations.

You are a solo blogger or site owner on a tight budget

Start with Ahrefs. Use Ahrefs Free on your own site first. It's free forever and shows your backlinks and technical issues. When you need to research competitors and keywords, move to Starter at $29 a month. You'll outgrow it eventually, but it is the cheapest real step up from free.

You are a freelancer doing SEO for a few clients

It's close. Lean Semrush if you report a lot. Semrush SEO and Ahrefs Lite cost about the same. Semrush gives you daily tracking for 500 keywords and easy client reports. Ahrefs Lite gives you more tracked keywords, API access and that excellent link data. Try the Semrush trial, use Ahrefs Free on a site you own, and pick the one you enjoy using.

You run a small agency that sells more than SEO

Choose Semrush. If your clients pay you for SEO, PPC, local and reporting, Semrush covers all of it with one login. Start on Pro+ for 15 sites and 1,500 tracked keywords, then add toolkits client by client.

You run a link building or digital PR shop

Choose Ahrefs. Backlink research is the job, and that's Ahrefs' home ground. Standard gives you unlimited credits, Content Explorer and Batch Analysis for prospecting at scale.

You care most about AI search visibility

Lean Semrush on value. Daily prompt tracking is built into Starter and Pro+. Ahrefs includes a smaller monthly allowance of prompt checks, and its Brand Radar research tool is a separate purchase. Both are moving fast here, so check the latest plan pages.

You are a developer or data team

Choose Ahrefs. API and MCP access from Lite is the cheapest way to pipe serious SEO data into your own tools or AI workflows.

Frequently asked questions

Is Semrush better than Ahrefs?

Neither is better for everyone. Semrush is the better all in one marketing platform, with SEO, AI visibility, ads, content, local and social tools you can add as you grow. Ahrefs is the better choice if your work centers on backlinks, competitor research and fast, clean SEO data.

Which is cheaper, Semrush or Ahrefs?

Ahrefs has the cheaper entry point. Its Starter plan is $29 per month, and Lite is $129 per month. Semrush starts at $139 per month for the SEO plan, or $117.33 per month billed annually. At the mid tier the gap closes: Semrush Pro+ is $299 per month and Ahrefs Standard is $249 per month.

Do Semrush and Ahrefs have free plans?

Both do. Semrush Free gives you 1 demo project and 10 reports per day with no credit card. Ahrefs Free gives free, limited access to Site Explorer and Site Audit for websites you can verify that you own.

Do Semrush and Ahrefs offer free trials?

Semrush offers a 7 day free trial on its SEO and AI Search plans, although data exports are switched off during the trial. Ahrefs does not offer free trials or discounts. Its free plan is the closest thing to a test drive.

Which has the bigger backlink database?

It is hard to say fairly, because each vendor counts its data differently. Semrush says its backlink index has more than 43 trillion backlinks, updated daily. Ahrefs says it runs the second most active web crawler after Google and refreshes its backlink index every 15 to 30 minutes. Test the same domains in both before you decide.

Which is better for a small agency?

Semrush usually fits small agencies better if you sell a mix of services such as SEO, PPC, local and reporting, because its toolkits and white label reports cover more ground. Ahrefs Standard fits agencies that mainly sell SEO and link building, and it includes unlimited credits.

Can Semrush and Ahrefs track AI search visibility?

Yes. Semrush paid plans include AI visibility features, and Starter and above add daily prompt tracking, starting at 50 prompts. Ahrefs includes tracked AI prompt checks from Lite upward and sells Brand Radar as a separate tool for wider AI visibility research.

Should I buy both Semrush and Ahrefs?

Most people should not. The overlap is large. Pick one as your main tool. If you need a second opinion on links, the free Ahrefs plan for your own sites, or the cheap Ahrefs Starter plan, can sit next to a Semrush subscription without a big extra cost.

Final recommendation

For most marketers and multi service agencies, Semrush is the better choice. It scored 8.3 out of 10 in our Semrush review. It does SEO well, and it also covers AI visibility, ads, local, social, content and reporting. You can start with SEO and add the rest only when you need it.

Ahrefs is the better choice if SEO is your whole job, especially links. It is cleaner, it is cheaper to start, and its data is a joy to dig through. Standard is the sweet spot, with unlimited credits and the research tools pros lean on.

Still unsure? Test both for free. Start a Semrush trial and set up Ahrefs Free on a site you own. Look up the same three competitors in each. Then keep the one you find yourself opening without being asked.
