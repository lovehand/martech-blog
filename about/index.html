Why Martech.blog exists

If you have ever tried to pick a CRM or an email platform, you know the feeling. You search for "best marketing automation software" and find ten lists that all recommend the same tools in a slightly different order. Half of them read like the vendor wrote them. None of them tell you what it is actually like to use the thing on a Tuesday afternoon when a campaign is due.

Martech.blog was started to fix that. The idea is simple: sign up for the software, use it for real marketing work, and then write the review we wish we had found when we were buying. Plain language. Clear verdicts. No pretending every tool is perfect for everyone.

We are not a news site. You won't find funding announcements or press releases here. Everything we publish is meant to help you make a buying decision, whether that is your first CRM or a replacement for a platform your team has outgrown.

The martech challenge

There are now well over ten thousand marketing technology products. That number sounds impressive until you are the person who has to choose one.

The problem isn't a lack of options. It is a lack of trustworthy guidance. Feature pages promise everything. Pricing pages hide the real cost behind "contact sales". Review sites are often shaped by who pays for placement. And the cost of a wrong choice is high: lost months of migration, frustrated teams, and contracts you can't easily leave.

Our view: buying software should feel like getting advice from a friend who has already used every option. That is the standard we hold ourselves to.

Who we help

Our readers tend to be people who have to make a call and live with it. That usually means:

  • Founders and small business owners picking their first real marketing stack on a tight budget.
  • Marketing managers and heads of growth who need tools that work together and prove ROI.
  • Marketing operations and RevOps teams evaluating integrations, data quality and scalability.
  • Agencies and consultants who recommend software to clients and can't afford to be wrong.

If you are trying to work out which tool fits your team, your budget and the way you actually sell, you are in the right place.

Our research process

Every review, comparison and buying guide follows the same process. It takes longer than rewriting a vendor's feature list, and that is the point.

  1. We define the buyer. Before testing, we decide who the page is for: team size, budget, use case and technical skill.
  2. We sign up and set up. We create real accounts, usually on free trials or paid plans, and configure them the way a new customer would.
  3. We run real workflows. Importing contacts, building an automation, sending a campaign, creating a report and connecting common integrations.
  4. We verify pricing and facts. Plans, limits and features are checked against official vendor pages and documentation, and we record the date.
  5. We compare against alternatives. No tool is reviewed in isolation. We judge it against the products a buyer would realistically consider instead.
  6. We review and update. Pages are revisited regularly, and any time a vendor makes a meaningful change to pricing or features.

Editorial standards

  • Independence. Vendors cannot pay for a ranking, review a draft or approve a verdict.
  • Accuracy. We cite sources, link to official pricing and correct mistakes quickly and openly.
  • Freshness. Every major page shows the date it was last updated.
  • Balance. Every review lists real pros and cons. If we can't find a downside, we haven't tested hard enough.
  • Clarity. We write for busy people. Short sentences, real examples, and a verdict near the top.

You can read the full details in our editorial policy.

Affiliate transparency

Martech.blog is free to read. To keep it that way, some links on the site are affiliate links. If you click one and buy, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Here is what that does not mean. It does not mean a tool ranks higher. We recommend products we don't earn anything from, and we criticize products we do. If a commission ever influenced a verdict, the whole site would stop being useful, including to us.

Where we're heading

Our goal is to become the most trusted place on the internet to research marketing software. That means more hands on reviews, more honest head to head comparisons, and buying guides that stay current long after they are published.

Over time we plan to add original data from our testing, buyer surveys and practical templates you can use when evaluating tools yourself. If there is a tool or comparison you'd like us to cover, tell us. Reader requests shape what we test next.

Frequently asked questions

Is Martech.blog independent?

Yes. We are not owned by any software vendor, and vendors cannot pay for placement or approve our reviews.

Do you actually test the software you review?

Yes. We create accounts and use each product on real marketing tasks before publishing a review or ranking it in a buying guide.

How do you make money?

Mainly through affiliate commissions when readers buy through some of our links. This never changes our rankings or verdicts.

How often is content updated?

Major buying guides are reviewed regularly, and any page is updated when a vendor changes pricing or features in a meaningful way. Each page shows its last updated date.

Can vendors request a review?

Vendors can suggest a product, but they don't control whether we review it, what we write or how it scores.
