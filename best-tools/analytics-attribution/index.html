2. Google Analytics 4: best free analytics and attribution

Our score: 8.2 / 10. GA4 is the default analytics tool for most websites, and for good reason. It costs nothing, it connects to Google Ads in a few clicks, and its data driven attribution model is genuinely useful. It just won't see sales that happen outside your site.

Overview

Google Analytics tracks how people find and use your website and app. Google describes the standard version as free of charge. Large organizations can upgrade to Google Analytics 360, which raises limits on things like event parameters, audiences, explorations and BigQuery exports. 360 is sold on contract, so pricing is quote based.

For attribution, GA4 now offers three models in its Attribution reports: data driven attribution, paid and organic last click, and Google paid channels last click. Google removed first click, linear, time decay and position based models in November 2023. That surprises a lot of people who learned on the old Universal Analytics.

Key features

  • Data driven attribution. Google says it uses machine learning on both converting and non converting paths, looking at factors like time, device and the order of ad exposure.
  • Attribution paths. See the sequence of channels people touched before a key event.
  • Consent mode and behavioral modeling. GA4 can model the behavior of users who decline analytics cookies, based on similar users who accept them.
  • Free BigQuery export. Export raw events to BigQuery. Standard properties have a daily export limit of 1 million events, and BigQuery storage and queries may cost extra.
  • Google ecosystem. Native links to Google Ads, Search Ads 360, Display & Video 360 and Search Console.
  • Explorations. Funnel, path and segment analysis for deeper questions.

Analytics and attribution buying guide

Most attribution regret comes from one of three mistakes. Buying a tool that can't see where your sales happen. Trusting one model as if it were the truth. Or underestimating how much setup clean tracking takes. Here is how to avoid all three.

Analytics vs attribution, in plain English

Analytics answers "what happened?" How many people visited, where they came from, what pages they read and who signed up. Attribution answers a harder question. "Which marketing deserves credit for this sale?"

Think of it like a soccer goal. Analytics tells you a goal was scored. Attribution decides how much credit goes to the striker, the player who passed to them, and the defender who started the move.

Attribution models explained

Let's use one simple journey. Sam sees your LinkedIn ad. A week later Sam reads your blog post from Google. Then Sam clicks your email and books a demo. Three touchpoints. Here is how each model hands out the credit.

ModelHow it worksSam's demo creditGood for
First touchAll credit goes to the first interaction.LinkedIn ad 100%Seeing what creates awareness and new leads.
Last touchAll credit goes to the last interaction before converting.Email 100%Short, simple purchases. Seeing what closes.
LinearCredit is split equally across every interaction.About 33% eachA fair, simple view of longer journeys.
Time decayTouchpoints closer to the conversion get more credit.Email most, blog some, ad leastSales cycles with a clear decision phase.
Data drivenAn algorithm compares paths that convert and paths that don't, then assigns credit based on what actually seems to make a difference.Depends on your dataBusinesses with enough conversion volume to train the model.

A few things worth knowing. First touch and last touch are simple, but they ignore everything in the middle. Linear is fair but treats a quick page view the same as a demo request. Time decay rewards whatever happened last, which can undervalue brand building. Data driven models are the most sophisticated, but they need volume. With only a handful of conversions a month, the model has little to learn from.

Tools also label these differently. GA4 now offers only data driven and last click models. HubSpot offers first touch, last touch, linear, time decay and its Empirical model. Mixpanel adds participation, U shaped and J shaped. Ruler adds position based plus data driven and impression attribution.

Our advice is simple. Don't pick one model and treat it as gospel. Compare two or three. A channel that looks strong in first touch, linear and data driven is almost certainly pulling its weight.

Attribution vs MMM vs incrementality

Multi touch attribution follows individual journeys. It is great for day to day campaign decisions, but it misses anything it can't track, like a podcast ad or a billboard. Marketing mix modeling, or MMM, uses statistics on total spend and total sales over time, so it can include offline channels. Incrementality tests, such as holdouts and geo lift tests, switch marketing off for a group and measure the difference. Triple Whale Enterprise, Northbeam and Ruler's Advanced plan all offer some form of MMM, which tells you where the category is heading.

Privacy, cookies and server side tracking

Tracking has gotten harder. Cookie consent banners, browser privacy features and ad blockers all reduce what you can see. That is not a bug in your tool. It is the new normal, and you should plan for it.

  • Consent comes first. In many regions you need permission before setting analytics or advertising cookies. Use a proper consent management platform and make sure your tools respect its choices.
  • Modeled data fills some gaps. GA4's behavioral modeling for consent mode estimates the behavior of users who decline cookies, based on similar users who accept them. It is helpful, but it is an estimate, not observed data.
  • First party data is gold. Form fills, logins, CRM records and orders are data you own. Tools like Dreamdata, Ruler and HubSpot are strong partly because they tie back to these records.
  • Cookieless tracking exists, with limits. Dreamdata, for example, lists cookie and cookieless tracking on its free plan. Ask any vendor exactly what "cookieless" means for its product and how it handles consent.
  • Server side tracking improves control. Instead of loading many tags in the visitor's browser, you send data to your own server, which forwards it to tools like GA4 and ad platforms. Google says server side tagging in Tag Manager can improve site performance and security. It runs on Google Cloud by default, which has a free tier but may incur costs.
  • Server side is not a consent loophole. It changes how data travels. It does not change whether you are allowed to collect it. Keep your consent rules in place and talk to your legal team.

How to choose attribution software

  1. Find where your revenue is recorded. Online checkout points to ecommerce tools like Triple Whale. A CRM points to Dreamdata, HubSpot or Ruler. In app upgrades point to Mixpanel.
  2. Map your journey length. One visit purchases can live with simple models. Three month B2B deals need multi touch and account level views.
  3. Check your data volume. Data driven models need enough conversions. If you have very few, start with rule based models.
  4. List your must have integrations. Ad platforms, CRM, ecommerce platform, call tracking and data warehouse. Confirm each one is native.
  5. Start free where you can. GA4, Dreamdata, Triple Whale and Mixpanel all have free plans. Use them to check your tracking before you pay.
  6. Get the total price in writing. Platform fee, onboarding, add-ons and how pricing changes as traffic, events or revenue grow.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best marketing attribution software?

For most B2B companies, Dreamdata is the best marketing attribution software because it ties ads, content, website visits and CRM deals together at the account level and has a free plan to start. Ecommerce brands should look at Triple Whale first. If you have no budget yet, start with Google Analytics 4, which is free and includes data driven attribution.

Is Google Analytics 4 good enough for attribution?

For many small businesses, yes. GA4 is free and offers data driven attribution plus two last click models. Its limits show up when your sales happen offline or in a CRM, when deals take months to close, or when you need first touch, linear or time decay models, which Google removed in November 2023.

What is the difference between marketing analytics and marketing attribution?

Marketing analytics tells you what happened, such as how many people visited, where they came from and what they did. Attribution goes one step further and assigns credit for a conversion or a sale to the touchpoints that came before it, so you can see which channels actually drive revenue.

Which attribution model should I use?

Start with the model that matches your sales cycle. Short, single visit purchases are fine with last touch. Longer journeys are better served by linear, time decay or a data driven model. The most useful habit is to compare two or three models side by side and look for channels that score well in all of them.

How much does attribution software cost?

It ranges from free to thousands of dollars a month. GA4, Mixpanel, Dreamdata and Triple Whale all have free plans. Ruler Analytics lists indicative pricing from $400 a month, HubSpot Marketing Hub Professional is $800 a month with a $3,000 onboarding fee, and many advanced attribution plans are quote based. Pricing checked November 2026.

Do cookie banners and ad blockers break attribution?

They reduce what you can see. When visitors decline cookies or block scripts, you lose some touchpoints. Tools respond with consent aware tracking, modeled data, first party cookies and server side tracking. None of these fully replace lost data, so treat attribution numbers as strong directional evidence rather than perfect truth.

Do I need a CRM to use attribution software?

Not for ecommerce, where the sale happens on your website. For B2B and lead based businesses, yes in practice. Revenue attribution needs to connect a lead to a closed deal, and that deal lives in your CRM, such as HubSpot or Salesforce.
